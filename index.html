About Me

My first encounter with computers dates back to age 12, when I started programming in BASIC with the help of my uncle—back when screens were green and cathodic tubes were still a thing. From early on, curiosity was my default setting. Although I was interested in many topics, I eventually gravitated toward studying Physics in Valencia (Spain), driven by a desire to understand how Reality actually works.

That curiosity later took me to Lausanne, where I was invited to pursue a PhD at EPFL within the Faculty of Electrical Engineering. There, I had the privilege of working on multi-physics simulation software for safety-critical systems—such as space satellites and particle accelerators—implemented in C, C++, and Fortran, where software bugs are more than just an inconvenience.

I then spent four years on CNES-funded projects in France, continuing to model and develop software to predict the behavior of electronic devices inside satellite payloads while operating in the harsh in-orbit environment. Together, these experiences laid a strong foundation in software architecture, complex numerical algorithms, and professional, client-oriented project management.

In 2020, I decided to put my technical and managerial skills at the service of data-driven projects, with a particular focus on the healthcare domain. Since then, I have contributed to several initiatives delivering digital solutions that help business analysts and researchers make better decisions by improving both the quality and availability of their data. I have designed and implemented automated pipelines to extract, transform, and deliver data reliably—turning raw data into something people can actually trust and use.
